The big dates and ideas from Latin American history, in order.

  1. Before 1492

    Three great civilizations

    • Maya city-states in Mesoamerica, such as Tikal, built pyramids and kept detailed calendars and writing.
    • Aztec empire centered on Tenochtitlan, a city built on islands in a lake (founded 1325).
    • Inca empire in the Andes, with its capital at Cusco, thousands of miles of roads, and records kept on knotted cords called quipus.
  2. 1492

    Columbus and the Columbian Exchange

    • Christopher Columbus reaches the Caribbean, opening permanent contact between Europe and the Americas.
    • The Columbian Exchange moved corn, potatoes, tomatoes, and chocolate to Europe, and horses, wheat, and sugar to the Americas.
    • European diseases also crossed the ocean, and Indigenous populations fell sharply.
  3. 1519–1533

    The conquest

    • Hernán Cortés defeats the Aztecs and takes Tenochtitlan in 1521; Francisco Pizarro defeats the Inca in 1533.
    • Weapons and horses helped, but so did alliances with groups that resisted Aztec and Inca rule, and above all disease.
    • Spain built a colonial empire ruled from Mexico City and Lima, with forced labor systems like the encomienda.
  4. 1500s–1700s

    Colonial life and mixing of cultures

    • Catholic missions spread Spanish language and religion across the colonies.
    • Indigenous, Spanish, and African peoples lived, worked, and married together, creating mestizo culture.
    • This blend shaped Latin American food, music, art, and the Spanish spoken today.
  5. 1804–1822

    Independence

    • Haiti becomes the first Latin American nation to win independence, in 1804.
    • Mexico begins its fight in 1810 with Father Miguel Hidalgo and wins independence in 1821.
    • Simón Bolívar and José de San Martín free much of South America in the 1810s and 1820s; Brazil separates from Portugal peacefully in 1822.
  6. 1900s

    Revolution and modern nations

    • The Mexican Revolution starts in 1910; the Panama Canal opens in 1914.
    • The Cuban Revolution brings Fidel Castro to power in 1959.
    • Military dictatorships in the 1960s and 1970s gave way to elected governments in the 1980s and 1990s.
  7. Today

    Latin America now

    • More than 30 countries and territories make up Latin America and the Caribbean.
    • Spanish is the official language almost everywhere except Brazil (Portuguese), Belize, Guyana, and Suriname.
    • Writers like Gabriela Mistral, Pablo Neruda, and Gabriel García Márquez are read around the world.

Key words

indígena
native people of the Americas
colonia
a territory ruled by another country
mestizo
of mixed Indigenous and European heritage
independencia
freedom from colonial rule
revolución
a major, often violent, change in government
quipu
Inca recording tool made of knotted cords
